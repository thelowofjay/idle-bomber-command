- Visibility of system status: **3/4** — sortie state, 2× speed, force, bonds, priority target, and debrief are visible; purchase and audio-state changes are less clear.
- Match between system and real world: **2/4** — the airfield world is convincing, but the maintenance symbol, synthetic grenade/fighter audio, and ambiguous currency grouping break the fiction.
- User control and freedom: **3/4** — pause, retreat, closable dialogs, and mixer controls are strong; the sortie continues invisibly under the upgrade dialog.
- Consistency and standards: **3/4** — paper, olive, brass, and vermilion form a coherent system; some icon and hierarchy choices drift.
- Error prevention: **3/4** — confirmation exists for destructive reset; first-load sound activation and live-combat modal behavior are not explained.

## Design specificity verdict

The game now feels authored for Idle Bomber Command rather than like a generic mobile idle dashboard. The airfield plate, brass title, field-order card, department dock, battlefield, and debrief all belong to the same world. The main weakness is no longer visual identity; it is state choreography. Audio, dialogs, labels, and rewards sometimes describe a different state from the one the simulation is actually in.

## What works

1. **The main-menu identity is finally coherent.** The brass lettering, airfield lineup, squadron identity, field order, and department dock read as one product.
2. **The battle is understandable without a tutorial overlay.** Airfield at the bottom, defenses at the top, active force status, war bonds, upgrades, pause, retreat, and 2× status are all discoverable.
3. **The debrief visually lands well.** It is compact, legible, and clearly separates temporary sortie results from the persistent command screen.

## Priority issues

### [P1] First-load audio is silent and mission-end audio does not relinquish control

**Evidence:** The first seven seconds of the recording are digital silence at roughly **-91 dB**. After returning from the mission, the menu sits around **-21 dB**. Immediately before debrief, audio averages **-19.3 dB**; the first four seconds of debrief still average **-21.0 dB**. That is only a 1.7 dB reduction, not a transition.

**Why it matters:** The opening has no atmosphere, while the end of the sortie has no emotional punctuation. The Retreat call cannot own the result screen.

**Fix:** Add a one-time `Tap to enter command` sound-unlock action. At operation end, stop accepting new combat cues, quickly fade and terminate every active music/engine/weapon/explosion source, wait about 100–200 ms, then play Retreat alone. Start menu music only after returning to base.

### [P1] Enemy-fighter audio is detached from the aircraft and from 2× simulation

**Evidence:** The recording runs the whole sortie at 2× speed, while the fighter radial is a fixed six-second one-shot at normal cue timing. It does not follow position, approach, egress, or removal.

**Why it matters:** The sound feels strange because it is not behaving like the object on screen. It can also survive into debrief.

**Fix:** Make the fighter engine a tracked loop owned by the fighter entity. Pan and attenuate it with screen position/distance, modestly alter pitch across approach and egress, scale its lifecycle with simulation speed, and stop it when the fighter exits or crashes.

### [P1] Tactical dialogs obscure a simulation that keeps running

**Evidence:** `Sortie Upgrades` covers most of the meaningful battlefield while the operation remains in `playing`. The recording opens it during live combat.

**Why it matters:** Aircraft can take damage and priority opportunities can expire while the player is reading costs. That violates the game's promise that active decisions are optional rather than traps.

**Fix:** Either auto-pause while the tactical dialog is open or keep a visible `Combat continues` status with live threat/priority information outside the dialog. Auto-pause is cleaner for this prototype.

### [P1] Progression semantics contradict one another at debrief

**Evidence:** The debrief reports `Aircraft knocked out: 2` and `Aircraft recovered: 0`, then says aircraft are recovered after every operation; the main menu immediately shows `2 × KITE FLIGHT` again.

**Why it matters:** The player cannot tell whether `recovered` means landed safely, repaired, or retained permanently. Trust in the economy suffers.

**Fix:** Rename the rows to `Aircraft disabled this sortie` and `Aircraft returned safely`; change the explanatory sentence to `Disabled aircraft are repaired before the next operation.` Reserve `recovered` for the survival-bonus calculation only if its meaning is made explicit.

### [P2] The top balance strip visually misassigns numbers

**Evidence:** Each balance uses icon + expanding label + number across an equal-width column. The medal value sits near the trophy icon, and the trophy value sits near Settings.

**Why it matters:** Currency is the core progression language. A player should never need to trace which zero belongs to which icon.

**Fix:** Treat each balance as a tight `icon + number` object with the label directly below or immediately beside it. Hide Boss Trophies until they unlock, or show a clear `Locked` state instead of a permanent unexplained zero.

### [P2] Target durability is not teaching the progression ladder

**Evidence:** Foxholes, artillery, and bunkers are distinguished mainly by small sprites and thin health bars. At phone size and 2× speed, the player sees impacts but gets little feedback explaining why one target died and another survived.

**Why it matters:** The whole early progression depends on learning `one grenade kills a foxhole; artillery takes two`. That lesson currently remains hidden in the arithmetic.

**Fix:** On the first few impacts, briefly show restrained feedback such as `FOXHOLE DESTROYED` or `ARTILLERY DAMAGED · 8/16`, then stop showing tutorial copy once learned. Keep it spatial and short.

## Persona red flags

**First-time idle player:** Hears silence on arrival, sees a reserved boss currency with no use, and receives no explanation that 2× is active. They may misread silence as broken audio and the trophy zero as missed progression.

**Optimization-focused player:** Cannot easily learn target breakpoints from the battlefield, and the debrief's knocked-out/recovered wording makes survival-bonus math look inconsistent.

**Short-session mobile player:** Loses roughly a tenth of the screen to browser chrome because the recording is not running as an installed PWA. The tactical upgrade dialog can also hide live threats during a brief interaction.

## Minor observations

- Replace the Maintenance mark with one conventional wrench silhouette. The current crossed-tool symbol does not read immediately.
- The priority truck/ring can be partly clipped near the right edge when the alert first appears. Delay the alert until the target is meaningfully visible or inset its interaction bounds.
- Aircraft tags are acceptable with two planes, but the design will not scale to the intended 20 individual aircraft. Introduce collision-aware labels or show labels only for selected/damaged aircraft before expanding the fleet.
- Mixer sliders provide no useful preview for Engines, Weapons, or Explosions while on the menu. Play a short representative sample after the user stops dragging each bus.
- The game is installable, but the recording shows persistent browser chrome. Add a restrained installation prompt after the first completed operation, not on first launch.
- The lower battlefield is visually quiet for long periods while most action compresses into the upper-right quadrant. A second attack lane or more lateral route variation would improve fleet readability.
